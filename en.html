Privacy Policy – Start Force One

Version 4.2 · Last updated: 18 September 2026 · App: Start Force One (com.startforce.one)

1. In short

  • Everything you record stays on your phone. Videos (no sound), athletes and results are stored on the device. We run no servers, never see your data, and the app contains no advertising, analytics or tracking.
  • Nothing leaves the phone except: Android's backup of the app data to your Google account (if enabled), your photo app's cloud sync of the videos, files you export or share, the Pro check with Google Play at every start, and the two-phone transfer you set up (a connected finish phone sends its videos to the start phone).
  • Two-phone timing sends the finish video directly to your second phone — never through the internet or a server.
  • Google Play is contacted only to check or process the optional Pro purchase.
  • You are responsible for consent from the people you film — for minors from a parent or guardian (older minors who understand what they agree to may consent themselves where your law allows).

4. Where the data is stored

Results, athletes, teams, tracks and settings are stored in the app's private database on the phone. Videos are saved to the phone's gallery (folder Movies/StartForce) so that you can view and manage them like any other video. The pose detection (MediaPipe) runs entirely offline on the device. The app also keeps its diagnostic logs, finish videos waiting to be transferred and copies of your exports (for up to 24 hours) in its private storage; on Android 8 and 9 the videos go to the phone's general video folder instead of Movies/StartForce. There is no storage on servers of the developer.

5. What can leave the phone

  • Android backup. If device backup is enabled in Android, the system backs up the app's database and settings (the whole database — athletes, teams and notes, tracks, all run data including the tracked body points — and the settings including the name of this phone and the pairing code — not the videos and not the purchase status) to the Google account of the phone, restores them after a reinstall and copies them when you set up a new phone from this one (Android Auto Backup / device transfer). This is an Android feature controlled by you: Android Settings → Google → Backup (wording varies by manufacturer). The backup copy remains in your Google account after you uninstall the app until Google expires it or you delete it (Google One / Drive → Backups). On Android 9 and later this backup is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock — provided a screen lock (PIN, pattern or password) is set — so Google cannot read it. Google provides the backup as part of your Google account under its own terms; the developer has no access. If you act for a club, check whether backing up athlete data into a private Google account — for which no data-processing agreement exists — is compatible with your obligations, or turn the backup off.
  • Your photo app. Because the videos are stored in the gallery, a photo app with cloud sync (e.g. Google Photos) may upload them according to its own settings. Turn off backup for the folder Movies/StartForce there if you do not want that.
  • Your exports. PDF reports, raw-data CSV files, the app's backup archive (Settings → Backup & Restore) and the diagnostic logs (Help → Crash Log / Sync Log; the sync log may contain the local IP address of your second phone) are handed to the app you pick in the Android share sheet. From there, what happens to them is up to you. PDF reports contain the athletes' names, and the CSV file name (and the file name of a single-athlete PDF) carries the athlete's name. Exported files are not encrypted by the app — store and share them accordingly. Publishing recordings or reports (e.g. on social media or a club website) additionally requires the athletes' — or parents' — agreement under image-rights law (in Austria § 78 UrhG), independent of data-protection consent.
  • Two-phone finish timing. The finish video and the details needed to match it (run number, race distance, timing signals and the name of each phone — the phone model, or the name you set under Settings → Two-Phone Finish → Name of this phone; no athlete names) are sent directly from one phone to the other, either over a Wi-Fi Direct link or over a local Wi-Fi network both phones are joined to. No server and no third party is involved. While a session is active the start phone announces itself (service name, port and a random session token) on the Wi-Fi network or, over the direct link, to phones in radio range, so the finish phone can find it. The start phone stores the finish phone's name with each run, so it ends up in the start phone's backup; the finish phone keeps its own copy of every finish video in its gallery — if that phone belongs to someone else (e.g. a co-coach), that person receives the finish videos. Over Wi-Fi Direct the link is encrypted by Android (WPA2); over a shared Wi-Fi network the app adds no encryption of its own, so the data is only as protected as that network — avoid open or public networks. An optional pairing code (Settings) ensures that only phones with the same code can connect — without one, any phone running this app on the same network can; the code itself is never transmitted in clear text.

6. Google Play (optional Pro purchase)

Pro is a one-time purchase processed by Google Play (in the EEA: Google Commerce Ltd. / Google Ireland Ltd.; see the Google Play Terms) and tied to the Google account on the phone. Each time the app starts, it asks Google Play whether Pro is unlocked and for the current price; Restore purchase asks again, and Buy opens Google Play's purchase flow; for this the app needs internet access. Google sees the purchase and your Google account — never your recordings, athletes or results. Payment data is handled exclusively by Google under Google's privacy policy; the developer receives no payment details. The purchase status is stored locally on the phone. Your three free runs are counted on the phone only; that counter and the purchase status are excluded from the backup.

8. No third-party processing, no tracking

The app uses no advertising IDs, no crash or usage analytics and no cloud AI. The only third party it communicates with is Google Play for the Pro purchase (Section 6). Android's backup (Section 5) is a system service you control, not a transfer by the app.

9. Recording other people (club and youth context)

When you record athletes — especially minors — you as the user (coach, club) must inform them in advance and obtain their consent, and handle the recordings in line with the data protection law that applies to you. Tell athletes and parents in writing who you are, what you record and why, how long you keep it, whether it is backed up to your Google account, and that they may ask for access or deletion at any time (Art. 13 GDPR). For minors, obtain consent from a parent or legal guardian; depending on the law that applies to you, older minors who understand what they agree to (in Austria generally from 14) may consent themselves — when in doubt, involve the parents. Consent counts only if it is genuinely voluntary: athletes or parents must be able to decline or withdraw without any disadvantage for training, selection or team membership — offer an alternative such as training without recording. Body weight requires the explicit consent of whoever may consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR) — we recommend obtaining it in writing. Keep recordings of minors only as long as coaching needs them. The app shows a consent reminder on the first launch on each phone.

10. Children

The app is aimed at coaches and athletes, not at children as independent users. Where data of minors is recorded, Section 9 applies.

11. Retention and deletion

You decide how long data is kept. To remove everything: (1) delete runs, athletes and teams in the app; (2) delete the videos in the gallery (Movies/StartForce); (3) delete any PDF, CSV or backup files you exported; (4) if Android backup was on, delete the app's backup copy in Google One / Drive → Backups; (5) if you used a second phone as finish camera, delete the finish videos there too (its gallery and Finish Camera → Waiting to send). Uninstalling the app deletes its database and settings from the phone, but not the videos in the gallery, the exported files or the Google backup copy.

12. Your rights

Athletes exercise their rights — access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), objection (Art. 21) and withdrawal of consent — towards the coach or club who made the recordings, because only they hold the data. Consent can be withdrawn at any time with effect for the future. Towards the developer these rights apply to the purchase and support data described in Sections 2 and 6; for purchases we hold no name, only an order number — to exercise your rights regarding purchase data please quote your Google Play order number (GPA.…) from the Play receipt, otherwise we cannot identify you (Art. 11(2) GDPR). Everyone has the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority — the one of your own country of residence, or in Austria the Austrian Data Protection Authority (DSB), Barichgasse 40–42, 1030 Vienna (dsb.gv.at).
